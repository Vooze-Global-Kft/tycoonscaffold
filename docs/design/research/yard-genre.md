# The genre the scrapyard enters

Research date: 25 November 2026; every page below was read that day. [n] marks a source (list at the end). **Inference** marks my own reasoning. Wikis, guides, forum posts, store reviews and press are individual reports, not facts about a game. Fandom wikis and the Supercell help pages could not be read directly (empty or refused); where I use them I say "search extract". Where I write from memory I say so. This file does not repeat `genre.md` (production-chain idle sims, hires, prestige, ads) or `meta.md` (chests, missions, deals, day-1 hooks).

## 1. The hands-on resource tycoon

### 1.1 The reference: a lumber tycoon on Roblox

Lumber Tycoon 2 (Defaultio) is old and still alive: trackers report about 1.3 billion visits, published July 2009, last updated April 2026, a peak of about 9,000 concurrent players in October 2024 [1]. A fan blog calls it "one of those classic games that just doesn't seem to die" [2].

**What the hands do, minute by minute** (one guide [3]):
1. Walk from spawn to the tool shop, buy the cheapest axe, walk to a tree.
2. Hold the button on the trunk until it falls; the trunk lands as a physical log.
3. Drag the log by hand to the sell point beside the shop, or buy a small truck, load the logs, drive.
4. Buy a plot of land; buy a sawmill; feed logs into it; planks come out. Planks sell "for several times the log price" and stay on your plot between sessions [3]. A blockage warning appears when the output is full [3].
5. Load planks into the truck, drive to the sell point, sell, buy a better axe, sawmill or vehicle.

**Progress** [3][4]: about six axe tiers (some wood needs a good enough axe); a sawmill ladder; vehicles with more load; land (more plots, a whitelist for friends [5]); wood by region, from spawn to a snowy mountain, a volcano, a swamp, a ferry island and a cave that needs a special axe and a traded item [4]. Frost wood goes "from $9 as a log to $106 as a plank" [3]. Event items come only from past events or other players [3].

**What keeps players** (individual reports): the grind in "a peaceful environment"; the journey ("long drives", trucks that tip over); trading rare wood and axes, which duplication cheats "absolutely wreck"; purists "enjoy the grind of driving to the Frost Mountain and hauling wood back" [2]. **Inference:** the hook is physical ownership (your logs, truck, plot) plus a visible ladder of places you cannot yet work.

**Session length.** No figure for this game. On Roblox as a whole, games of 0–6 minutes per session see D1 below 10 %; hits sit at 19+ minutes [6]. Arcade idle reports D0 playtime of 25 minutes [7]. **Inference:** long sittings, unlike the 6–8 minute idle sessions in `meta.md`; helpers must make short sessions worthwhile too.

**Other players:** a shared server; visitors on your land under a whitelist; gifts of money in the same server [5]; trading; showing off a base.

### 1.2 Cousins

- **Mining on Roblox** (Mining Simulator 2, guides only [9]): mine until the backpack is full, walk back up, step on a sell pad. Two numbers matter, "your pickaxe's breaking power and your backpack capacity" [9]; pets from eggs; rebirth for a permanent multiplier; themed worlds with their own ores. Other mining tycoons combine mining with factory floors that refine ore [10].
- **Fishing on Roblox** (Fisch, guides only [11]): catch, sell, buy better rods, sail to farther islands; a per-island bestiary ("rarity, preferred bait, weather, season, and time of day") is the long goal [11].
- **On PC**: Hydroneer: dig, sort on conveyors, smelt into ingots, sell, automate; "Very Positive" from about 27,000 Steam reviews; criticised for a "very slow start" [12]. Junkyard Simulator: machines turn junk into raw materials; "Mixed", bugs [13]. On phones, Idle Lumber Empire (AppQuantum) is the tap idle form, 100M+ downloads [14].

**Inference:** three upgrades recur everywhere (tool power, carrying capacity, trip speed), plus a collection log and a map whose far edges hold the valuable material.

## 2. The same hand on a phone: arcade idle

**The loop.** "Players control a stickman avatar using visual joystick controls" [8]; resources are picked up by walking near them and carried as a visible stack; "stacking is another popular mechanic" [8]; players "collect resources, sell them, and use the money to unlock new zones" [7]; "each new zone brings them more profit but it gets more expensive to unlock" [8]. Upgrades "increase capacity, efficiency, or production speed" [8]; some games let players "hire workers" [8]. Zones open by walking onto a price tile on the ground and standing there while coins drain into it (memory: the standard look of My Perfect Hotel and its kind; none of the pages read today described it).

**Genre numbers (publisher and vendor figures, self-interested):** D1 about 35 %, D0 playtime 25 minutes, CPI 40 cents (Homa via ironSource [7]); top 20 D1 48–52 % (MAF [22]); "almost 50% day-1 retention" (Udonis [8]). Homa (paraphrased by the fetch tool): retention is often lower than in other idle games because play is more active; and "You can't lose in games like Harvest It, Craft Island, or Farm Land" [7].

**How they earn.** Rewarded videos replace "pay to go faster"; IAPs are permanent versions of the bonuses [7]. No Ads costs $9.99 (My Perfect Hotel [23]), $4.99 (Junkyard Keeper [17], Trash Collector [21]), $3.99 plus "AD SKIP TICKET" packs up to $39.99 (Scrapyard Magnate [19]), $12.99 with a $4.99 permanent 2x boost (Idle Junkyard Tycoon [16]).

**What players say about forced ads** (store reviews, exact):
- "The forced ads man… They're driving me crazy." (My Perfect Hotel, 20 Nov 2023) [23]
- "There r 2 many mf ads ... every few minutes." (Scrapyard Magnate, 15 Aug 2024) [19]
- "The ads for 'inventory and machine speed' pop up every 30 seconds which is INSANE." (Trash Collector) [21]
- "when you forced them on an individual three times before five minutes is ridiculous." (Junkyard City Scrap Tycoon, 16 Jan 2025) [24]
- Certain buttons are full ads, including a daily chest that "is not actually free." (Idle Recycle, 5 Nov 2022) [25]

**Why players leave** (reviews): "this game runs out of goals really quickly" (Junkyard Keeper, 4 Jan 2024) and "gets boring after a while" [17]; "the exact same as many other idle games" (Idle Recycle) [25]; a progression wall where vehicles are under-levelled for a stage (Idle Junkyard Tycoon) [16]; "no zone 4?" (Trash Collector) [21]; heat and lag on long play [17]. An industry playbook reads a strong D7 with a weak D30 as "a mid-game content wall" [26].

**Orientation.** All the arcade idle scrap titles checked today show portrait store screenshots (392×696 thumbnails) [18]. **Inference:** the genre is portrait-first because one thumb drives the joystick from the bottom of the screen.

**Scrap, metal and mining titles on phones** [16]–[21][24][25]. Steered: Junkyard Keeper (43,000 ratings), Stone Miner, Scrapyard Magnate, Trash Collector, Recycle Empire, Scrape Master ("automated trailers"). Tap idle: Idle Junkyard Tycoon, Scrapyard Tycoon, Scrap Metal Factory (city to city, landscape shots), Metal Empire ("Smelt, extrude and market your metal"), Mining Empire, Idle Recycle (grants, research), Junkyard City Scrap Tycoon. None names real alloys or processes, simulates two exchanges, or ships by sea, rail and air in its store text [18]. **Inference:** that space is open.

## 3. The business layers, as games do them

**Prices that move.**
- Phone idle games put a "market" in the loop as a timing choice: Oil Tycoon: Idle Miner Factory sends you to a stock market where you "monitor prices and make sure you sell up at the optimum moment" (TouchArcade summary); Oil Era sells to "the factory with the highest bid" [27]. No page described their price rules.
- The best-known timing market in a casual game is Animal Crossing's turnips: buy once a week, the sell price changes "every half-day", and the week follows one of "4 turnip price patterns" [28]. **Inference:** a small set of recognisable patterns lets players learn to read the market; pure noise does not.
- Design writing: Soren Johnson (2009) argues for markets as "a transparent mechanic within the game itself", spelled out ahead of time "so that no one feels the game is biased against them", citing Power Grid's visible resource track [29]. An Assassin's Creed concept uses tiered moves (usually ±5 %, sometimes ±15 %, rarely ±30 %) and news that shifts the odds (search extract [29]).
- **Inference for fairness:** show history and the next known event; let metal be stored at a visible cost; offer fixed-price contracts as the safe alternative to spot (hedging without derivatives); never move a price between showing and selling (brief, lesson 2); no real feed, nothing that reads as advice (L4).

**Contracts and partners.** Sim Companies: sell on an exchange or through contracts (from level 5), bonds from level 10; NPC companies "provide game-essential resources to the exchange" [30]. Transport Tycoon Empire: City collects trains, trucks, ships and planes "to complete transport contracts" [31]. Hay Day's set-price truck and boat orders are the casual form (memory).

**Choosing transport.** Transport Tycoon on iOS is "a nightmare when things go wrong on a small screen"; a Simutrans port is "AWFULLY unplayable" because controls are too small [31]. **Inference:** one decision with three visible numbers per mode (time, cost, capacity), not route drawing.

**Sites in other regions.** Idle chains do this as a new location with a fresh economy (`genre.md` 3); Metal Empire expands "first in one country and then all over the world" [18]; Salvage Empire seizes rival yards [18] (avoid: we buy, never seize).

**Listing the company.** I found no phone idle game that makes going public a mechanic; the search returned only real studio IPOs [32]. Offworld Trading Company (PC): 10,000 shares per company in blocks of 1,000; owning all of a rival's shares takes over its assets; buyout rules changed several times [33]. AdVenture Capitalist's "angel investors" are a reset currency (memory). **Inference:** make the listing the milestone that turns company value into the public score and opens simulated shareholders (expected dividends, the owner's stake as a choice); no share trading between players, which would read as real trading (L4) and need trusted clients (L5c).

## 4. Other players

**Group sizes** (official pages unless marked):
- Hay Day neighbourhood: up to 30 members (third-party guides, search extract [34]); weekly Derby "starts from 8 AM UTC on Tuesdays and ends at 8 AM UTC the following Monday" (Supercell help, search extract [34]); once in a Derby "you should not leave your Neighborhood until the Derby ends" [34].
- Township co-op: maximum 30 (fan wiki, search extract [35]); weekly Regatta in which members complete tasks to sail the co-op's yacht; leaders can bar members from it [35].
- Gardenscapes teams: "increased to 50"; "up to 3 co-leaders"; leadership "automatically transferred if the Leader hasn't been active for 14 days", to a co-leader or else "the longest-standing member"; "Simply entering the game is still counted as inactivity"; a weekly team score from "beating levels, sending lives, growing the team, and performing well in team events", with rewards to members who contributed [36].
- Royal Match: "A team can have up to 50 members"; members "communicate and give lives" [37].
- Eatventure clubs: two-week seasons (`genre.md` 4); clubs set their own minimums and remove members who miss them (Reddit, individual reports [38]).

**What a group of up to 30 does** (Inference from the above): one weekly shared goal fed by normal play (Derby, Regatta); small gifts or help requests; a ranking against similar groups; rewards only to contributors; a leader, co-leaders, automatic transfer when inactive. Chat is common but not required (L5d: none unless the policy research carries it). 30 is the Hay Day and Township size.

**Leaderboards and fairness.**
- Duolingo leagues: weekly groups of about 30, matched with people "who have similar study habits" and "a similar time zone" (official blog); top ranks move up, bottom ranks down (zone sizes vary by source) [39].
- Complaints in idle events (Steam forums, individual reports): a player in a bracket jumping "from 1k - 900k"; the top 30 "playing THIS current event for 5 DAYS when the event only just started"; players asking that developers "kick these people out" [40]. Fishdom's help notes a fast climb can be legitimate after reconnecting from offline play [40].
- **Inference:** fair boards are (a) weekly, in brackets of similar company size and start date; (b) on a score the server can recompute or bound (L5c), offline gains capped; (c) on what purchases do not buy directly (tonnage handled, contracts filled); the all-time "largest supplier" board kept separate.

**Visiting.** Hay Day: visit a friend's farm and buy from their roadside shop (older guides [41]); Lumber Tycoon 2: visitors on your plot under a whitelist [5]. **Inference:** a read-only visit (yard, plants, territories, logo, numbers) with one positive action (a like or small gift); no stealing, no harm.

**Complaints** (individual reports): leaders who kick members for missing points [38]; disputes over automatic leader transfer (Clash of Clans, search extract [42]); cheaters atop event brackets [40]; dead groups (memory), which the Gardenscapes rule that only playing counts as activity [36] addresses.

## 5. Landscape on phones

- Roblox: "the default Roblox setting is LandscapeSensor, where the game always appears in landscape mode (no portrait mode)" (Roblox docs, via search) [43]. Players of the owner's reference therefore know the lumber loop in landscape, with a dynamic thumbstick on the left and jump/action on the right (memory of the standard Roblox mobile layout).
- Phone idle and tycoon games are mostly portrait: in today's 1,055 store results for 14 keywords, 801 games' first screenshot is portrait and 254 landscape; of 474 games with "idle" or "tycoon" in the title, 62 have a landscape first screenshot [18] (the first image may be an iPad shot; Idle Miner Tycoon, a portrait game in my memory, is among the 62). Landscape among the titles checked: Car Mechanic Simulator, Pocket Planes, Game Dev Tycoon, Scrap Metal Factory, Factory Inc [18].
- Hoober's field study (2013, over 1,300 people): 49 % held the phone one-handed, 36 % cradled, 15 % two-handed; of one-handed users 67 % used the right thumb [44]. A browser game reports portrait games are finished "up to 30% faster" (self-reported) [45]. A GameAnalytics article says portrait "may be fine with idle games" [45].
- **Inference:** landscape loses one-handed, standing-up play. Both thumbs rest on the lower corners; the top middle is out of reach. Joystick lower left (appears where the thumb lands), the one context action (hold to cut, load, unload) lower right, money and goal at the top as read-only, menus from the right edge; support both landscape directions. Business screens (exchange, map, plants) are lists and charts that suit the width.
- **Store screenshots:** Apple accepts landscape at the swapped sizes (2868×1320, 2796×1290 for 6.9-inch) [46]. **Inference:** they show as wide tiles; the first must read at that size: the worker cutting a wreck in a wide yard beside the truck, one caption.

## For the designers

**Ten things to do**
1. Make the first minute physical: walk to a wreck, hold to cut, see the pieces stack on the worker, drive, unload, see coins. One thumb on a joystick, one on an action.
2. Grow three numbers the player feels: cutting power (what can be cut), carrying capacity (stack and truck), trip speed. Show each on the worker and the truck.
3. Put the valuable material farther away and behind a better tool: steel near the gate, copper and brass inside appliances, precious metals and tungsten in harder items, batteries handled at a separate safe bay.
4. Let helpers automate one step at a time, so the hand is never removed, only freed.
5. Give one ladder visible from minute one (L6): tonnage or company value toward "largest supplier", with the next milestone named.
6. Make the market readable: a few recognisable price patterns, a history line, known upcoming news, storage with a cost, and fixed-price contracts as the safe choice.
7. Make transport one decision with three numbers per mode (time, cost, capacity), each mode unlocked by the business, not route drawing.
8. Make the company group a weekly shared goal fed by normal play, with rewards only to contributors, co-leaders, and automatic leader transfer after inactivity measured by play.
9. Rank weekly in brackets of similar companies; keep the all-time board separate; compute or bound every ranked number on the server.
10. Use the landscape width: wide yard in play, lists and charts in the business screens; controls only in the lower corners.

**Ten things to avoid**
1. Any name, item, place or rule in the table in 1.3 (Lumberland, Wood R Us, a SOLD sign that pays, sell pads and egg pets, desert tow-truck yards, rival crews at the gate).
2. A crusher-on-wheels that mines by ramming (Stone Miner) or a vacuum that hoovers scrap (Trash Collector).
3. Forced ads, buttons that are secretly ads, "free" chests behind an ad (sections 2, reviews).
4. A zone or stage the player's vehicles cannot clear without paying (the Kolibri review).
5. Running out of goals after the first zones ("runs out of goals really quickly").
6. A market that is pure noise, or a price that changes between showing and selling.
7. Share trading between players, or anything that reads as real trading, betting or advice (L4).
8. Taking over other players' yards or any harm to another player.
9. All-time leaderboards as the only board; ranks a client reports.
10. Portrait habits in landscape: controls at the top middle, tiny route-drawing controls, small text in wide panels.

**The three biggest risks of a steered worker in a deep business game**
1. **Two games that fight for the same minute.** The hand wants long sittings (19+ minutes on Roblox, 25 minutes D0 in arcade idle [6][7]); the business wants short decisions and checking back. If business screens interrupt the hand, or the hand is needed for the business to earn, both suffer. Mitigation (Inference): the business opens only after helpers can run the yard alone, and every business decision can be made in under a minute.
2. **Depth that a phone cannot show.** Metals, alloys, processes, two exchanges, partners, four transport modes, territories, a listing and a guild is a PC-strategy amount of system; the arcade idle audience "can't lose" and leaves when confused. Mitigation (Inference): one new system per stage, each introduced through the yard (a partner's truck arrives, a ship docks), each with one decision and a default.
3. **The theme is crowded and close neighbours are recent.** Salvage Empire (Oct 2026), Kolibri's Idle Junkyard Tycoon (2025), Junkyard Keeper and Stone Miner already hold "scrapyard + truck" on phones, and the stores reject look-alikes (L8, `spam.md`). Mitigation (Inference): lead the store page and the first screens with what no one else has (the named metals trade: furnace, leaching line, ingots, exchanges), keep our own art and words, and check every name against 1.3.
