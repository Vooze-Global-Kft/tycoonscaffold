## 1. What comes into a yard

**Sources** (Memory unless marked):

- **End-of-life vehicles (ELVs).** Mostly steel; plus aluminium wheels and blocks, copper wiring, a lead battery, a catalytic converter [27], and in EVs a lithium-ion pack.
- **White goods** (fridges need gas removal first): thin "light iron" with copper motors.
- **Cable and wire:** copper or aluminium in PVC; sold as insulated wire (priced by recovery) or stripped and chopped.
- **Demolition steel:** beams, rebar, plate ("heavy melting steel").
- **Swarf / turnings:** oily chips from machining (steel, brass, aluminium, titanium).
- **Industrial offcuts ("new" or "prompt" scrap):** clean, known alloy; the best feed, usually under contract (§7).
- **Electronics (WEEE / e-scrap).** Circuit boards hold gold, silver, palladium and copper at a far higher grade than ore: "40 to 800 times" the gold in a tonne of US ore [28]. One review puts the yield of a tonne of mobile phones at about 130 kg copper, 3.5 kg silver, 0.34 kg gold and 0.14 kg palladium [28].
- **Batteries.** Lead-acid: an established, almost closed loop. Lithium-ion (phones to EV packs): a fire hazard, discharged and shredded into "black mass" (§3, §4). NiMH and alkaline in small amounts.
- **Catalytic converters.** A ceramic honeycomb coated with PGMs. One unit holds about 3–7 g platinum, 2–7 g palladium and 1–2 g rhodium [27]. That figure comes from press and dealer explainers and varies widely by car; diesel units are platinum-heavy and petrol units palladium-heavy [27].
- **Carbide tools.** Worn drill bits, inserts and saw tips made of tungsten carbide with a cobalt binder. Small by weight, high value [9].
- **Aircraft breaking:** aluminium alloys, titanium, nickel superalloys (Memory).
- **Ship breaking:** steel plate in huge tonnage, copper-nickel, bronze propellers; asbestos and oils removed first.

**Grades in plain words.** The trade uses the ReMA (formerly ISRI) *Scrap Specifications Circular*. Its nicknames go back a century [22]:

| Plain name | Code word | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Bare bright copper | Barley | Clean, bare, unalloyed wire. The best copper grade [22] |
| No. 1 copper wire / heavy | Berry / Candy | Clean unalloyed wire, or solids and tube [22] |
| No. 2 copper | Birch / Cliff | Mixed unalloyed copper, nominally 96% Cu [22] |
| Old sheet aluminium | Taint/Tabor | Clean mixed old alloy sheet [22] |
| Shredder aluminium | Twitch | Aluminium recovered from car shredders [22] |
| Shredded mixed non-ferrous | Zorba | The non-magnetic mix that comes out of a shredder [22] |
| Used beverage cans | UBC (Taldack and others) | Cans (Memory for the code word) |
| Heavy melting steel | HMS 1 & 2 (sold "80:20") | Thick old steel, ≥6 mm; the 80:20 mix is the world's benchmark grade [7] |
| Shredded steel | Shred (No. 1 shredded) | Fist-sized steel from the shredder (Memory) |
| Busheling / new production | No. 1 busheling | Clean factory offcuts of steel sheet (Memory) |
| Stainless 18-8 | 304 solids / turnings | 18% chromium, 8% nickel [14] |

## 2. What a yard does, machine by machine

All rough proportions are **Inference/Memory**. Real capital costs vary by a factor of three between countries and suppliers.

| Machine | In | Out | Cost tier | What it earns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Weighbridge** | Every truck, weighed loaded then empty | Net weight, ticket, seller ID (a legal record, §9) | Low | Nothing directly. Without it there is no trade |
| **Handheld XRF / LIBS analyser** | A piece of metal | The alloy grade in seconds | Very low (cost of a car) | Turns "mixed" into a named grade worth 10–50% more |
| **Radiation portal** | Every truck at the gate | Alarm or pass [26] | Low | Protects the business (§9) |
| **Oxy-fuel torch, plasma cutter** | Large steel | Furnace-size pieces | Very low | Labour-heavy; the first-hour tool |
| **Alligator / mobile shear** (excavator-mounted) | Beams, plate, frames | Cut HMS | Medium | Fast cutting of heavy steel |
| **Baler / logger** | Light steel, cans, aluminium | Dense bales | Low–medium | Cheaper freight, a better price |
| **Cable stripper** | Thick cable | Bare copper + insulation | Very low | Lifts insulated wire to No. 1 price |
| **Cable granulator** | Thin mixed cable | Copper "nuggets" (granules) at about 99.9% + plastic fluff | Medium | Same, for wire too thin to strip |
| **Car depollution rig** | ELV | Fluids drained; battery, catalyst, tyres, airbags removed | Low | Valuable parts set aside; a legal requirement in many places |
| **Shredder (hammer mill)** | Car bodies, white goods, light iron | Fist-sized pieces | Very high (the big step up, often tens of millions; Memory) | Scale: hundreds of tonnes a day |
| **Overband / drum magnet** | Shredder output | Ferrous shred vs. the rest | Low (comes with the shredder) | Sellable shred |
| **Eddy-current separator** | Non-magnetic stream | Zorba (aluminium, copper, zinc) vs. "fluff" | Medium | Recovers the non-ferrous |
| **Sensor sorters** (X-ray, XRF, colour, LIBS) | Zorba | Twitch aluminium, copper, brass, stainless, zinc | High | Turns Zorba into pure grades |
| **Heavy media plant** (sink-float) | Zorba | Heavy metals vs. light aluminium | High | An older alternative to sensors |
| **Battery discharge and dismantling** | Li-ion packs | Discharged modules, then cells | Medium | Makes shredding safe; the step into black mass |
| **Battery shredder (inert atmosphere)** | Discharged Li-ion | Black mass + copper and aluminium foils + steel + plastics | High | Black mass is priced as a "payable" (§3) |
| **Catalyst decanning** | Converter | Steel shell + ceramic "cat powder" | Low | Powder is sampled, assayed, sold to a PGM refiner |

**Inference.** Every step is mixed → sorted → pure → refined, each raising the price per tonne; machines map one to one onto steps.

From steel scrap to iridium that is a factor of about 800,000, too wide to show plainly. A game can show it on a log scale, or (simplified) let each rung be worth about 3–10× the one below it.
- Iridium and rhodium are **by-products** of platinum mining. That is why they are scarce and jumpy (§5). In scrap they appear in grams, never in tonnes: a converter holds 1–2 g of rhodium [27]. **Inference:** the game should show iridium in grams or ounces, coming from spark plugs, crucibles and electrolyser stacks bought from industrial partners.
- Black mass is not a metal. It is a black powder (graphite + nickel/cobalt/manganese/lithium oxides), sold by assay [11].

## 4. Production: furnaces and chemistry

The process names below are standard metallurgy (Memory). The simplifications are noted in place.

### Pyrometallurgy (heat)
- **Induction furnace:** electric coil, clean and fast; copper, brass, bronze, steel, precious metals → ingots.
- **Reverberatory furnace:** flame over a shallow bath; big batches of aluminium and copper.
- **Rotary furnace:** tilting drum; dirty aluminium under salt flux, lead battery paste; by-product **salt slag / dross** (recycled).
- **Electric arc furnace (EAF):** electrodes melt 100% steel scrap; by-products **slag** (road aggregate) and zinc-rich **EAF dust** (to Waelz kilns).
- **Smelting:** melting with reductants and fluxes; precious metals from e-scrap and catalysts gather in a copper or lead "collector".
- **Converting:** blowing oxygen through melt → "blister" copper.
- **Fire refining:** → anode copper (≈ 99.5%).
- **Casting.** Ingots (remelt metal), **billets** (logs for extrusion), **anodes** (plates for electrorefining), **T-bars/sows** (large aluminium blocks) [4].
- **Off-gas cleaning.** Bag-house **filters** catch dust (zinc and lead oxides, which are sold). This is the owner's "filters" on the pyro side.

### Hydrometallurgy (water and chemistry)
- **Leaching:** dissolving metal in acid (sulphuric for black mass; chloride routes for gold and PGMs).
- **Filter press:** squeezes slurry into metal-rich solution and solid cake (graphite, silica); the owner's "filters" on the hydro side.
- **Solvent extraction (SX):** an organic liquid picks one metal (cobalt, nickel) out in mixer-settler tanks.
- **Ion exchange:** resin columns polish traces or win precious metals from dilute streams.
- **Precipitation:** a reagent makes a solid crystallise: lithium carbonate, cobalt hydroxide, nickel sulphate, gold powder.
- **Electrowinning:** current plates metal out of solution (copper, cobalt, nickel cathode).
- **Electrorefining:** an impure anode dissolves, pure metal plates out: copper "Grade A cathode", the LME grade [4]. The **anode slime** collects gold, silver, PGMs, selenium, tellurium: one of the richest by-products in the industry.

### Chains a game can show as machines in a row
(→ main product; [+] by-product worth selling. Real chains, simplified.)

1. **Copper:** granulator → induction melt → fire refining → anode casting → electrorefining tank → **cathode** [+ anode slime → precious metals].
2. **Lead from batteries:** battery breaker → (acid neutralised, **plastic chips** sold) → rotary smelter → refining kettle → **lead ingot** [+ polypropylene, + sodium sulphate].
3. **Aluminium:** shredder → eddy current → sensor sort → rotary or reverb melt → alloying → **ingot / billet** [+ dross → recovered aluminium].
4. **Steel:** shear / shredder → magnet → EAF → ladle → continuous caster → **billet** [+ slag aggregate, + zinc dust].
5. **Precious metals from electronics:** dismantle → shred → smelter (copper collector) → electrorefine copper → **anode slime** → leach → precipitate → **gold, silver, palladium bars/sponge** [+ copper cathode].
6. **Black mass:** discharge → inert shredder → sieve → **black mass** → leach → filter press → SX (Co, Ni) → precipitation → **cobalt sulphate, nickel sulphate, lithium carbonate** [+ copper and aluminium foils, + graphite].
7. **Tungsten from carbide:** sort carbide → zinc process (zinc swells and frees the cobalt binder; zinc is distilled off and reused) → **reclaimed carbide powder**; or the chemical route → digestion → **APT** → oxide → **tungsten powder**. Memory: the zinc process is the usual recycler route.
8. **PGMs from catalysts:** decan → mill and sample → assay → smelt (collector metal) → PGM refinery (leach, ion exchange, precipitate) → **Pt, Pd, Rh sponge** [+ iridium and ruthenium in small amounts].

**Inference.** Beyond "assay", chains 5–8 are run by a few large specialist refiners; recyclers ship concentrate and are paid on assay (§7). Building these plants late is the game's "become the supplier" step, as the largest real groups do.

## 5. Prices and exchanges

### How an exchange of the London kind works (from the LME's own guide [4])
- **Metals.** Aluminium, aluminium alloy, NASAAC (an aluminium alloy), copper, lead, nickel, tin, zinc and cobalt are physically deliverable. There are cash-settled futures (settled against a price agency's index, no metal changes hands) for steel scrap (CFR Turkey, Taiwan, India; 10 t lots), rebar and HRC, cobalt, lithium hydroxide (1 t), molybdenum and the aluminium premiums [4] (checked).
- **Lots.** Aluminium, copper, lead and zinc trade in 25 t lots, aluminium alloy 20 t, nickel 6 t, tin 5 t, cobalt 1 t [4] (checked). Each lot must be an approved **brand** of a set quality, for example "Grade A copper" cathodes or "tin of 99.85% purity" [4].
- **Prompt dates.** "Daily: out to 3 months; weekly: 3 out to 6 months; monthly: 7 out to 123 months" for copper and aluminium [4]. **Cash** means delivery in two days and **3-month** is the benchmark forward (Memory for "two days").
- **Warrants and warehouses.** Delivery happens by a warrant, a title document for metal in an approved warehouse: "over 500 international storage facilities and almost 500 LME listed brands" (side panel: "over 570 storage facilities", "almost 500 LME-approved brands from 57 countries") [4] (checked). Published **stocks** show how much metal sits in those warehouses: on 24 Nov 2026 copper stocks were 337,825 t and tin 3,725 t [1]. Falling stocks usually mean rising prices.
- **Hours.** "The Ring | Trading hours: 11.40 – 17.00", open outcry in five-minute sessions; "LMEselect | Trading hours: 01.00 – 19.00", electronic; "Inter-office telephone | 24 hours" [4]. The **Official Price** is set in the second Ring session, 12.30–13.25 [4].
- **Contango / backwardation.** Forward above cash (contango) means plenty of metal. Cash above forward (backwardation) means a shortage now. On 24 Nov 2026 copper cash was $12,035 and 3-month $12,055 (mild contango), while zinc cash was $3,665 and 3-month $3,508 and aluminium cash $3,120.50 and 3-month $2,789 (backwardation; aluminium steeply) [1] (checked). **Inference:** the cash–forward gap is itself a game signal (shortage now vs. later).
- **Premiums.** The exchange price is for metal in a warehouse. A buyer pays a regional premium on top for metal delivered to them; the LME even lists premium contracts for US, West Europe and East Asia aluminium [4].
- **Limits.** In March 2022 nickel went from $48,078 to $101,365/t in early trading; the LME suspended trading and cancelled that day's trades [19].

### The Shanghai kind
- Futures in yuan, in **5 t lots** for copper; day sessions 9:00–11:30 and 13:30–15:00 Beijing time (third-party summary) [16]. **Daily price limits** change by notice: summaries give ±3% as a base, but a notice of 30 June 2026 set copper, aluminium, zinc and lead at "±9% from the settlement price of the previous trading day", nickel ±10%, gold ±14% [16]. **Inference:** the game's limit is a design choice. A night session (21:00–01:00) began for base metals in December 2013 [17].
- Metals: copper, aluminium, zinc, lead, nickel, tin, gold, silver, steel products (individual report; Wikipedia [17]).
- **Precious metals are not priced on the LME.** Gold, silver, platinum and palladium trade over the counter in London with daily benchmark auctions (Westmetall's "Gold London Fixing": $3,622.55 on 24 Nov 2026 [1]; market structure Memory). Rhodium, iridium and ruthenium have no exchange (below).
- **Why the two prices differ, and how they link.** Shanghai prices include Chinese VAT and import duty and are in yuan. Traders watch the **SHFE/LME ratio**. When Shanghai is high enough to cover freight, duty and currency, the **import window** opens: traders buy in London, ship to China, and the bonded-warehouse premium at Shanghai (the "Yangshan premium") rises [18]. In July 2026 a ratio near 1.14 (after currency) gave an import profit of about CNY 200/t [18]. **Inference:** this is a natural game mechanic. Two prices drift apart, and shipping metal between the two regions pays when the gap is larger than freight plus a fee.

### Metals that do NOT trade on such exchanges
Tungsten, titanium, iridium, rhodium, ruthenium, lithium carbonate and black mass are priced by **dealers and published assessments**. Price-reporting agencies call buyers and sellers and publish a range, for example "$2,900–3,250/mtu" for APT [8] or "73.3% payable" for black mass [11]. Refiners also publish their own posted prices: Umicore posts a daily "10am" price for iridium, rhodium and ruthenium [2][3]. **Inference:** minor and PGM prices should move in steps (posted price changes on some days and not others), not tick by tick. Umicore's iridium was unchanged at $10,200 on most days between 10 and 24 November 2026 [3].

### How scrap is priced off the exchange
- **Percentage of exchange ("payable", "coefficient").** No. 2 copper at 94.5–95.5% of LME, bare bright at 98.5–99% (Aug 2026, SMM) [12]. UBC at 50–55% of the US delivered aluminium price (Q1 2026) [13]. Black mass at 73.3% of contained metal value [11].
- **Discount in cents per pound below the exchange** (the US habit for copper; Fastmarkets publishes "No2 copper … LME/Comex discount, US cents per lb" [12]).
- **Formula = exchange price × metal content × payable − treatment charge.** Used for e-scrap, catalysts and black mass, with settlement after **assay** (Memory for the general formula).
- Ferrous scrap is not priced off any exchange. It trades in its own $/t market, with Turkey's HMS 80:20 import price as the world benchmark [7].

### What moves prices
- **Day to day:** Chinese data, the dollar, exchange stocks, strikes, tariff and export-rule news.
- **Over years:** building and car cycles, the energy transition, supply shocks. APT Rotterdam went from $900–940/mtu (Jan 2026) to about $3,000 (Aug), "nearly 500pc year on year" after China's export controls, while Chinese APT stayed near $1,008–1,025/mtu (Argus, 20 Aug) [8]: export rules can split one metal into two regional prices; lithium carbonate hit its 2026 low near CNY 90,000/t [10]; ruthenium rose 109% to $1,780/oz by 13 Oct 2026 [30].

**Inference for a simulator.** Daily move ≈ annual volatility ÷ √250. That gives about 1% a day for copper and aluminium (17% ÷ 15.8 ≈ 1.1%) and, as a guess with no measured source here, 2–3% for nickel, lithium and the minor metals. Add rare "events" of 20–100%, a slow mean-reverting trend, and step changes for posted prices.

### May a game use the real names and prices?
- **Data.** The LME's own policy: "The distribution and use of this data is permitted and controlled via various Market Data licences and procedures" [5]. Licence classes exist for real-time, 30-minute delayed and next-day delayed data [5][6], and a distribution licence for real-time or delayed data is quoted at "$25,000" a year in the LME's FAQ (search-result excerpt; the FAQ PDF itself was not read) [6].
- **Names and logo.** The LME guide states: "The London Metal Exchange logo is a registered trademark of The London Metal Exchange" [4].
- **The Shanghai exchange.** Its own licence and trademark terms could not be found or read in this session. Vendor pages show that its data is sold under licence through LSEG and ICE (individual reports) [17].
- **Inference (this matches L2).** Use no real exchange name, logo or price feed. Invented exchanges, with prices generated by the game's own model, need no licence. Prices "in true proportion" taken from this document are facts used to set tuning, not a redistributed feed.

**Inference.** On one consistent source, relative cost per tonne-km is about ocean 1, barge 2–3, rail 6, truck 28, air 210 (Krammer: air vs. ocean "a ratio of about 200"). That ordering, together with "what is worth flying" (Inference: only metals worth more than about $1,000/kg), gives a clear game rule: steel goes by ship, iridium goes by plane.

**Special cases.**
- **Scrap ships:** loaded by grab or magnet; checked for radiation and contamination at loading (Memory).
- **Batteries.** Lithium-ion batteries are "classified in Class 9 – Miscellaneous dangerous goods", as UN 3480 (alone) or UN 3481 (with or in equipment) (IATA 2026 guidance [25], checked). **By road, rail and sea**, damaged or defective cells travel under Special Provision 376, marked "DAMAGED/DEFECTIVE" (ICAO papers [25]; road and sea codes not read). **By air**, damaged cells that may overheat "are forbidden for transport by air" (IATA 2026, A154 [25]); waste and recycling batteries (A183) are forbidden "unless approved by the appropriate national authority of the State of Origin and the State of the Operator" (ICAO paper wording; IATA names A183, full text not read) [25]. Lithium-ion batteries flown on their own "must be shipped at a state of charge (SoC) not exceeding 30%" [25]. **Inference:** in the game, battery loads go by road, rail or sea, never by plane.
- **Precious metals:** armoured transport, vaults, specialist insurance, "valuable cargo" by air (Memory). E-waste across borders needs prior consent (§9).

## 7. Partners

All Memory/Inference: standard practice, simplified for a game.

- **Small suppliers.** *Peddlers* sell at the weighbridge at a posted board price, identified and paid electronically (§9). *Tradesmen* bring cable, pipe, lead. *Garages and breakers* bring converters, batteries, ELVs. *Demolition firms* get containers on site and a per-tonne price by grade. What keeps them: fair weight, fair grade, quick payment.
- **Large manufacturers.**
  - *Take-back / closed loop:* the maker's offcuts or returned products come back to it as recycled metal.
  - *Tolling:* the owner keeps the metal and pays a fee per tonne to refine it (normal for e-scrap, catalysts, black mass).
  - *Offtake:* the buyer commits to a tonnage over years at a formula price; this finances new plants.
  - *Certified recycled content:* chain-of-custody records, third-party audits, schemes such as ISO 14001, ISO 45001, R2 / e-Stewards (Memory).
  - *Audits:* of legality, material origin, safety, data destruction.
- **Buyers.** EAF mills (ferrous), remelters (aluminium), smelters and rod mills (copper), foundries (brass, bronze, cast iron), PGM and gold refiners (e-scrap, catalysts, slime), battery-material plants (black mass), traders (anything; they carry the price risk).

**Inference.** Partner tiers: local contractors → regional firms → large fictional brands whose multi-year contracts require certifications, audits and a minimum tonnage.

## 8. Growing, "the largest", and listing

- **The real path** (Memory/Inference). One yard with a torch and a baler → a shear and a weighbridge → a car-depollution licence → a **shredder** (the big capital step) → several feeder yards around one shredder → a port terminal for export (deep-sea bulk) → downstream: an aluminium remelter, a copper granulation plant, a battery shredder → acquisitions in other regions and countries → a refinery or EAF mill. Large groups grow mostly by **buying other yards**.
- **What "largest" is measured by.** Tonnes handled or sold (the trade's main number; Sims Limited reported 6.3 Mt of proprietary sales in FY26 [29]). Also revenue, number of sites, countries, and market value if listed. **Inference:** the game's one ladder (L6) can be "tonnes supplied". It is a figure a real recycler would put first, and every system (yards, plants, partners, transport) feeds it.
- **Listing, in plain words** (Memory, simplified). The company is split into **shares**; a **float (IPO)** sells some to the public for growth cash; **market value** = share price × shares. A listed company publishes audited **reports** each half-year and year, follows disclosure rules and keeps a minimum free float; its share price follows profits, growth and metal prices. **Inference:** in a game, listing = one cash injection plus a new score (market value), paid for by periodic "results" the player is judged on; plainly a simulation (L4).

## 9. The law and the dangers (show the yard as legal and well run)

**Stolen metal.** Cable, lead roofing, manhole covers and catalytic converters are classic theft targets. A responsible yard is licensed, verifies and records every seller's identity and keeps records (UK: at least three years [23]), and in the UK pays only by "a non-transferable cheque" or "electronic transfer", never cash (Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 s.12 [23]). Suspicious loads are refused and reported. **Inference:** show the ID check and electronic payment as routine; never offer "no questions asked".

**Hazardous waste and export.** Batteries, oily swarf, fridge gases, ELV fluids and airbags are hazardous waste, handled only on permitted sites. Since 1 January 2025 the Basel Convention requires the importing country's prior informed consent even for non-hazardous e-waste (new entry Y49 beside hazardous A1181; old B1110 deleted), for all Parties except Canada, Israel and New Zealand [24]. A good yard keeps consignment notes and ships only to licensed plants. **Inference:** exports can need a permit the player earns through certification.

**Battery fires.** Damaged lithium-ion cells can ignite days later; they are a leading cause of recycling-site fires (Memory). A responsible yard keeps a separate covered store with thermal cameras, discharges before shredding, shreds under inert gas and isolates damaged cells in drums (Memory; transport side [25]). **Inference:** show the battery bay and the drum, teach no technique.

**Sealed radioactive sources.** Medical and industrial devices sometimes reach scrap with the source inside; in 2010 a cobalt-60 source sold with old laboratory equipment killed a scrap dealer in Mayapuri, Delhi [26]. Responsible yards screen every truck with a portal monitor and follow IAEA SSG-17 [26]; on an alarm the load is isolated and the regulator called (Memory for the procedure). **Inference:** show the portal and "alarm → regulator collects it", never a handling minigame.

**Pressure vessels.** Gas cylinders, extinguishers, closed tanks and shock absorbers can explode in a shredder or furnace; yards refuse them unless certified degassed and opened (Memory).

**Fumes and acids.** Melting releases lead, zinc and cadmium fumes; battery and leach acids burn. Plants use extraction hoods, bag filters, protective equipment, neutralising stations, permits and air monitoring (Memory). **Inference:** chemistry stays inside the machine: no recipes, no concentrations.

## 11. Glossary (40 words)

1. **Anode.** A cast metal plate that dissolves during electrorefining.
2. **Anode slime.** The sludge left under refining tanks; rich in gold, silver and PGMs.
3. **APT.** Ammonium paratungstate, the salt in which tungsten is traded.
4. **Assay.** A lab measurement of exactly how much metal a lot holds.
5. **Backwardation.** Metal for delivery now costs more than later; a shortage signal.
6. **Bale.** A compressed block of scrap.
7. **Billet.** A cast log of metal for rolling or extrusion.
8. **Black mass.** The powder from shredded lithium batteries.
9. **Brand (exchange).** An approved producer's metal that may be delivered against a contract.
10. **Busheling.** Clean steel sheet offcuts from factories.
11. **Cash price.** The exchange price for prompt (near-immediate) delivery.
12. **Cathode.** A plate of pure metal made by electrolysis.
13. **CFR / CIF.** A price that includes freight (and insurance) to the buyer's port.
14. **Contango.** Later delivery costs more than now; a plentiful-supply signal.
15. **Depollution.** Removing fluids, batteries and hazards from an end-of-life car.
16. **Dross / slag.** The crust or waste layer on molten metal; often recycled or sold.
17. **EAF.** Electric arc furnace, which melts scrap into steel.
18. **Eddy-current separator.** A magnet rotor that flicks non-ferrous metal out of a stream.
19. **ELV.** End-of-life vehicle.
20. **Electrowinning.** Plating metal out of a solution with electric current.
21. **Ferrous / non-ferrous.** Containing iron (magnetic) / everything else.
22. **Filter press.** Plates that squeeze a slurry into liquid and solid cake.
23. **Fire refining.** Burning impurities out of molten copper.
24. **Granulator.** A machine that chops cable into copper granules.
25. **HMS 80:20.** Heavy melting steel, 80% grade 1 and 20% grade 2; the world benchmark.
26. **Ingot.** A cast block of metal for remelting.
27. **Leaching.** Dissolving metal out of solid material with a liquid.
28. **Lot.** The fixed quantity of one exchange contract.
29. **mtu.** Metric tonne unit: 10 kg of WO₃ (the tungsten pricing unit).
30. **Offtake agreement.** A long contract to buy a plant's output.
31. **Payable.** The share of contained metal value the buyer pays for.
32. **PGMs.** Platinum-group metals: Pt, Pd, Rh, Ir, Ru (and osmium).
33. **Premium.** An amount paid above the exchange price for delivered metal.
34. **Prompt date.** The day an exchange contract must be delivered.
35. **Shred.** Fist-sized steel from a shredder.
36. **Solvent extraction.** An organic liquid that picks one metal out of a solution.
37. **Sponge.** A porous metal (PGM or titanium) straight from refining or reduction.
38. **Tolling.** Refining someone else's material for a fee while they keep the metal.
39. **Warrant.** A title document for metal in an exchange warehouse.
40. **Zorba.** The mixed non-ferrous fraction from a shredder.
