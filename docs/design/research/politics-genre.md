# Research: the genre the satirical government tycoon enters

Read on 2026-11-23 with generic web searches, public App Store pages (US) and Google Play search pages. Google Play detail pages came back truncated (no reviews readable), so Play evidence is search-result order and star ratings only. Numbered sources are at the end. **Inference** marks my own reasoning; **(memory)** marks something I know from before this session and did not re-read; store reviews, forum posts and press are individual reports, not measurements. `genre.md` and `meta.md` already cover idle-tycoon loops, chests, deals and haptics; none of that is repeated here.

## 1. The neighbouring kinds

| Kind | Usual loop | Session | Earns by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Swipe decision card games** (Reigns, Lapse, Mayor Card) | one card, two answers, meters move; a meter at empty or full ends the reign; a new ruler starts | 2 to 10 min per reign; a few hours to see the whole deck [2][7] | premium price ($0.99 to $2.99) [1][7] |
| **B. Petition and court games** (Sort the Court, Yes, Your Grace) | visitors ask; yes or no; days and budgets | 10 to 30 min | free start, one unlock purchase [9][10] |
| **C. Paperwork and inspection games** (Papers, Please, Death and Taxes, Paperwork Inc) | check a document against today's rules, stamp; end-of-day money screen | 10 to 20 min per day | premium [11][12][13] |
| **D. President and nation simulators** (MA / President Simulator series, House of Power, President: Simulator Game, Suzerain, Democracy) | laws, budget, crises; text events | long; text-heavy | free with weekly subscriptions $4.99 and packs up to $39.99 [4][5]; Suzerain free prologue then paid [14] |
| **E. Political idle tycoons** (Pocket Politics 1 and 2, Idle Mayor Tycoon, The President, Politics Inc.) | buy and level income buildings ("lobbies"), hire managers, offline earnings, prestige | idle: short and frequent | rewarded ads, gold packs $1.99 to $29.99, VIP subscription [3][6][8][15] |
| **F. Election games** (The Political Machine, 270, Presidential Election Race) | spend money and time on a map of real states, debates, polls | one election 20 to 60 min | premium $2.99 plus purchases [16] |

### What players praise and complain about

- **A. Swipe cards.** Praise: easy to pick up, humour, "depth" behind a simple input ("Amazing card game", Reigns US, 05/05/2025 [1]). Complaints: "it is to random" and a request to see whether each choice is good or bad (Reigns US, 10/17/2018 [1]); cards "very repetitive toward the end" (Steam forum [17]); repetitive "after about half an hour, even with new flavor text on events that more or less do the same thing" (Harvard Crimson review [17]); Lapse: "difficulty spikes can lead to rapid and frustrating game-over states" (aggregator [7]).
- **B. Petitions.** Yes, Your Grace is criticised for "withholding information that can very easily be given" (Pocket Gamer [10]); a GOG user had "little grasp as to the consequences of my decisions" [10]. Sort the Court players call it easy to pick up; one found the yes/no format tiring [9].
- **C. Paperwork.** Pocket Gamer on Papers, Please: "looks, feels, and plays like monotonous work - and it's fantastic" [11]; on phones it "does feel slightly cramped" (148apps [11]). Death and Taxes: repetitive, most of it seen "in about three runs" (Metacritic users, as summarised [12]).
- **D. President simulators.** The loudest complaints in this whole study are paywalls: "You can't even get through 10 minutes of gameplay without a paywall"; "No, I'm not going to pay five dollars a week" (House of Power, 12/24/2025 [4]); "by the time I'm elected I run out of money and can't play anymore" (09/06/2025 [4]); a funds wall that "requires spending money to progress" (President: Simulator Game, 2025/2026 [5]). The developer answered "We know the campaign and money balance feel too strict right now" [4]. Suzerain: "paragraph after paragraph", a request to skip texts already read because "reading the same texts over and over is becoming annoying" (store review [14]).
- **E. Political idle.** The President (Coco Play): "too many ads", ads "at least one minute long", levels "START TO REPEAT after about day 50", sexual content complaints, rated 13+ with "Sexual Content or Nudity" [3]. Pocket Politics: ads that fail to load make the game feel "unplayable", notifications "every ten seconds" [6]; Pocket Politics 2: missions that cannot be completed, and a reviewer who says the game "shouldn't make players pick a side" (Liberal or Conservative) [15].
- **Preachy or partisan.** The one explicit complaint found is the Pocket Politics 2 reviewer above [15]. **Inference:** the genre's own store pages show where the partisan risk sits: real countries and leaders in titles ("Prime Minister UK Politics Sim", "Trump Tycoon", "270 ... US Election" [18][19]), a real party axis [15], and dark-power fantasies ("silence critics, rig the ballot" [4]; "ruthless dictator" [5]) that push age ratings to 13+ with weapons and horror content [4][5].

## 2. The games to stay away from

Genre mechanics (yes/no on a card, meters, a desk, offline income) are free to use. Each line below is that game's own signature: a design that matches any one line must change.

- **Reigns series (Nerial / Devolver)** [1][2][20][21]. Name words: "Reigns", "Her Majesty", "Swipe and be the King". Rules: exactly **four meters** (church, people, army, treasury) where **both empty and full kill** the ruler; tilting a card shows the two answers on its edges (memory); small dots above the meters show which will move but not which way (memory; "a clue ... but it's not complete information" [21]); a years-in-power counter and a dynasty of successive rulers after each death; a hidden long-term goal across generations; a five-item inventory (Her Majesty [22]). Look: flat, faceless geometric portraits on a single card over a coloured background (art by Mieko Murakami [20]), chiptune score by Disasterpeace [20]. Characters: the Devil, the Spirit of the Fallen, the Senator, a skeleton [20]; a jester, a witch (memory). Medieval court.
- **Lapse (Stefano Cornago)** [7]: swipe cards with four statistics in a post-apocalyptic future nation; a time-loop plot.
- **Sort the Court! (Graeme Borland)** [9]: three buttons (yes, no, and a third), gold / happiness / population counters, a tiny pixel king on a throne receiving visitors one by one.
- **Yes, Your Grace (Noodlecake publisher; developer Brave At Night from memory)** [10]: a king's throne room queue of petitioners, a family and a winter siege, a weekly budget of supplies (family and siege from memory).
- **Papers, Please (Lucas Pope)** [11][23]: the invented country Arstotzka and its neighbours (Kolechia, Obristan); a border booth; passports checked against a daily rule list; fingerprints and scans; citations by fax; the **end-of-day family budget** (rent, food, heat), debt ends the game; drab pixel art; "Glory to Arstotzka" (memory); red and green entry stamps (memory).
- **Death and Taxes (Placeholder Gameworks)** [12]: you are a reaper at a desk deciding from portrait files who lives or dies; a boss named Fate (memory).
- **Democracy (Positech)** [24]: a screen-wide web of policy icons joined by influence lines, sliders for taxes and spending, coloured voter-group happiness readouts (layout from memory).
- **Suzerain (Torpor Games)** [14]: the invented country Sordland and its president (memory: Anton Rayne); long branching prose.
- **Pocket Politics 1 and 2 (Ultrabit / Kongregate)** [6][15]: income buildings called **lobbies**, "President of the World", "President For Life", a Liberal-or-Conservative choice; packs named "Offshore Account", "The Steal Deal", "Fake Media's Pack".
- **The President (Coco Play)** [3]: "idle mafia game", "become the world's president", dress-up clothing layers.
- **Hidden Lake Games' President: Simulator Game, House of Power, Prime Minister: Political Sim** [4][5][19]: Oval Office and White House, "Mr President", republic-or-dictator paths, "the election clock never stops", two-round runoffs.
- **MA / President Simulator / The President: 20th Century (Oxiwyle)** [18]: the "MA n" numbering, real world map and real nations.
- **Idle Mayor Tycoon (T-Bull)** [8]: hiring "leaders", stages from "a quiet green place" to "big metropolises".
- **The Political Machine (Stardock), 270** [16][18]: a real US electoral map, real candidates (memory), states and issues.
- **Paperwork Inc (Chensu Games)** [13]: a printer issuing papers to desks, approve or deny, a bribe-risk slider.

**Check for designers (Inference):** our desk must not show four meters that kill at both ends; must not be a single portrait card swiped left or right with answers on its edges; must not end the day on a family budget; must not call its buildings lobbies or its ruler "the King"; must not use a dynasty/years counter as the run score; no faceless geometric portraits, no pixel border-booth look. Our own shape (see `office-design.md`): approve or refuse via two buttons on a paper, several named voter groups, approval as a revenue multiplier, an election as the reset.

## 3. Written comedy at the length of a phone line

What is known:

- Reigns: Her Majesty's storylets are "each only a sentence or two long" [21]; its writer found a good card hard because of the limited space [22], and said "It's a whole bunch of modular moments that have to look like they're designed to go together" [22]. The reviewer "laughed out loud five or six times during my first 40 minutes" [21]: a rate, not every card.
- Reigns' designer: hints "that the apparent randomness of the cards was hiding something" make "every single card ... potentially a meaningful choice" [25]. The Devil storyline exists to explain why characters repeat (press summary of his GDC 2017 talk; the talk itself not read [26]).
- Reigns removes "all the cards that have been played too recently" and weights cards by the kingdom's state, so a war brings war cards and suppresses jester cards [27].
- Reading speed: adults read English non-fiction silently at 238 words a minute, most between 175 and 300 [28]. A 30-word paper is therefore 6 to 10 seconds of reading before the decision (**Inference**), which fits the brief's "several seconds per paper".

**Inference, from the above and the complaints in section 1:**

- **Forms that last a hundred papers:** a fixed frame with a variable absurd payload (the same committee always proposing a study of itself, each time a bigger study); a petitioner whose request escalates each time they return; dry official language describing something silly ("Proposal: rename Tuesday. Cost: 4 signs."); the player's own vanity as the target (statues, ribbons, a portrait); consequence lines that pay off a setup from the paper the player saw before.
- **Forms that wear out:** puns (funny once, groan on the second sight); pop-culture references (they date, and they point at real things); a catchphrase on every paper of a character; "random" jokes with no setup; shock or cruelty; anything whose joke is the policy itself, since the effect then reads as an opinion.
- **Running jokes:** a decision that returns 5 to 30 papers later naming what you chose ("You approved the giant duck. It has a union now."). This is the cheapest way to make the deck feel written for this player (Reigns' modular "designed to go together" [22]). Keep a flag per callback; never show a callback whose setup the player did not see.
- **Text budget:** one or two sentences, about 15 to 30 words on the paper, 3 to 6 words on each button, and one short line of outcome. Show effects as icons, not prose. A paper already seen should never be read again at full length (Suzerain complaint [14]); recently seen papers are removed from the draw [27].

## 4. Keeping approve-or-refuse more than a coin flip

- **The two failure poles found:** too random (Reigns review [1]; Yes, Your Grace hiding information [10]) and mindless juggling ("a mindless juggling of the four bars", a reader quoted in [2]; "the plot is just a quiz", a commenter in [21]).
- **Definition:** "An option that is so good it's never worth doing anything else is a dominant strategy" [29].

**Inference: what makes it a decision**

1. **Visible first-order effect, hidden or delayed second-order.** Show the budget change and which groups like or dislike it before the tap (the brief requires shown numbers to be the numbers applied); keep only the later callback unknown. Players forgive surprises that are stories, not surprises that are numbers.
2. **Opposing groups.** Every paper pleases at least one group and annoys another; refuse is never neutral, because the petitioner's group dislikes a refusal.
3. **Limited resources.** Approvals cost budget or political capital; a full yes-streak runs out of money, a no-streak loses approval and so revenue. Both must lose, measured by bot: always-approve, always-refuse and coin-flip should each land well below a reading player.
4. **State-dependent value.** The same paper is good when a group is unhappy and bad when it is content; the draw favours papers that answer the current state [27].
5. **Delayed consequences with a warning.** A returning paper is announced ("The duck committee is back") so the player can plan.
6. **No instant death from one card.** The genre's sharpest complaint is a run ended by a misread effect [21][7]; in an idle game, unpopularity should cost revenue and bring scandal papers, not wipe progress.

## 5. Store search

Read from Play search order and the titles seen (no keyword tool with an account was used; volumes are **Inference**).

| Term | Who holds it now | Crowding (Inference) |
|---|---|---|
| president simulator | Hidden Lake, Oxiwyle (five titles), a dozen "President Simulator: X" clones [18] | saturated; title pattern worn out |
| politics game / political sim | real-country sims, Suzerain, 270, House of Power [19] | crowded, realist and partisan-leaning |
| mayor / mayor simulator | city builders and three "Mayor Simulator" titles [30] | crowded with city builders; "mayor" alone reads as city building |
| idle ... tycoon | dozens of "Idle X Tycoon", "X Inc: Idle Tycoon" [31] | worn out (and `meta.md` already advises against "Idle ... Tycoon") |
| swipe decision / choices | Lapse, story-choice games, Yes, Your Grace [32] | moderate; mostly narrative games |
| satire politics | the same realist sims [19] | the word is not a title word anyone owns |

- **Worn-out title patterns:** "President Simulator: …", "Mayor Simulator …", "Idle … Tycoon", "… Inc: Idle Tycoon", "Prime Minister: …", "Election …" with a year.
- **Words that make the app look like news, official or partisan (Inference):** a real country or leader name; election years ("2024", "2026"); "election", "vote", "ballot", "campaign" as the first title word (reads as a civic or polling app); "government", "official", "ministry of", "senate", "congress", "parliament", "White House", "Oval Office"; "liberal", "conservative", "left", "right", "fake news", "rigged", "dictator", "corrupt" [4][5][15][19]; flags, seals or eagles in the icon.
- **Keyword ideas (Inference):** lead with a coined, invented title, then a genre subtitle built from words players use and the realist apps do not combine: "town hall", "mayor to president", "funny", "office", "desk", "decisions", "approve", "idle", "tycoon" (in the subtitle only), "cartoon". "Silly politics" / "funny politics" place the app as comedy. Keep "satire" out of the title (unowned but rarely searched, Inference) and use it in the description.

## For the designers

**Do**

1. Show each paper's budget and group effects as icons before the tap, and apply exactly those.
2. Make every paper please one group and annoy another; refusal always has a cost.
3. Prove by bot that always-approve, always-refuse and random each finish clearly behind a reading player.
4. Keep papers to one or two sentences (15 to 30 words) and buttons to a few words.
5. Write callbacks: a decision returns later naming what the player chose, and is announced first.
6. Remove recently seen papers from the draw; weight the draw by the current mood of the groups.
7. Make the player's own character the butt of the joke: vanity, ribbons, statues, committees.
8. Let unpopularity cost revenue and bring scandal papers, never wipe a run on one card.
9. Keep every paywall out of the decision loop: ads and purchases speed things up, never unlock the next paper.
10. Use a coined title with a comic genre subtitle; invented towns and parties in every screenshot.

**Avoid**

1. Four meters that kill at empty and at full; a dynasty or years counter as the score.
2. One portrait card swiped left or right with answers on its edges; faceless geometric portraits.
3. A border booth, passports, a daily rulebook, red/green entry stamps, an end-of-day family budget.
4. Buildings called "lobbies", "President of the World/For Life", Oval Office, "Mr President".
5. A left/right or liberal/conservative choice, or any party that maps onto a real one.
6. Dictator, rigged-ballot or "silence critics" paths; anything that needs a 13+ violence or horror descriptor.
7. Puns, pop-culture references and catchphrases as the main joke engine.
8. Long prose, or re-showing a full paper the player has already read.
9. Hidden numeric effects, or forced ads (reviews punish ads that are long or fail to load).
10. Titles with "President Simulator", "Idle … Tycoon", "Election", a year, a real country, or "government"/"official".
