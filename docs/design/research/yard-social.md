# Scrapyard: Groups of up to 30 in farm and tycoon games (the owner named Hay Day's neighbourhoods as the model)

Researched on 26 November 2026 for step "research-social", by reading pages today: the publishers' own help centres first (Supercell's Hay Day support portal, Playrix's Township and Gardenscapes help, Dream Games' Royal Match help, EA's SimCity BuildIt help), then two fan wikis (the Hay Day Fandom wiki and the Eatventure Fandom wiki, read through their public page-source interface), then trade press and analysts (PocketGamer.biz, mobilegamer.biz, GameRefinery, Naavik). Reddit and two help pages would not load (marked "search extract only"). Searches used only public game names and general words; nothing about this project went into any query. Every claim is marked **VERIFIED** (read today on the page named), **REPORTED** (a secondary source or a search extract) or **INFERENCE** (mine). Rules are described in my own words; no table, task list or text of another game is reproduced. Numbers are as read on 26 November 2026.

*Checked the same day by a second reader (step "check-social"): fifteen claims opened at their sources, corrections made in place, and the section "Checked" at the end lists what was found and added. ## The answer in brief

1. **What the owner pointed at is a group of up to 30 farms that share a weekly contest, ask each other for goods, and see each other.** Each member keeps their own farm; the group adds a building on every farm, a request board, a chat, and a weekly race against about 14 similar groups, with leagues. That fits our specification (section 7): each player keeps their yard; the company is the group.
2. **The weekly contest is what made the group matter.** Before the weekly race, neighbourhoods were "just a place to talk and perhaps do some trading" (Camilla Avellar, former designer, PocketGamer.biz [P1]); the race "changed the game completely" (Avellar, mobilegamer.biz [P2]); both REPORTED interviews of 21 June 2022, quotations checked on the pages. Our weekly order is the right centre.
3. **Its fairness comes from three rules worth having in our own form:** the roster is frozen when the week starts; groups are matched with groups that have the same number of *taking-part* members; and the goal per member is the same whatever the group's size.
4. **Its weak spots are known:** players kicked during the week lose their prizes (complaints); tasks are generated from the highest-level member (low-level members can be shut out); premium currency buys an extra task and reshuffles prizes, skips the wait between help requests, and buys extra tasks in the shared seasonal event, and the paid season pass sells one extra help request a day (all VERIFIED on the support portal [S3], [S4], [S10]); the roadside shop's price floor of 1 coin makes it a channel for trading for real money (resellers advertise delivery through it).
5. **30 is Hay Day's number (the publisher's own App Store text: "create your own with up to 30 players" [S0]); the trend has moved to 50.** Township's co-ops of 30 became "Teams" of up to 50 (the publisher's help states the 50; the old 30 and the date, version 30.0.1 of 11 September 2025, come from the fan wiki [W7]), Gardenscapes and Royal Match allow 50, SimCity BuildIt's clubs 25. The owner's 30 is a sound size.
6. **Do not build a player-to-player stand with free prices** (section 3 has its abuses; section 6 a safe form).

## 2. The weekly contest

### 2.1 The rules today (all VERIFIED on the support portal [S9]–[S12] unless marked)

- **When.** Tuesday 08:00 UTC to the following Monday 08:00 UTC; Monday is a 24-hour break.
- **Who.** Members at farm level 18 or above. During the Monday break a player opts in, opts out, or moves group; nobody joins a race after it starts. Inactive players are opted out automatically (REPORTED [W3]).
- **Leaving mid-race.** If you leave or are kicked, your points still count for the group, but you cannot claim prizes.
- **Tasks.** A shared board; each player takes one at a time, with a time limit and points; a missed timer gives nothing and still uses one of the week's tasks (REPORTED [W3]). Tasks are generated from the highest farm level among taking-part members; each member may take those at or below their own level.
- **How many tasks.** A per-player weekly allowance that depends on the group's league. Once a player has used their allowance, one extra task can be bought with diamonds; in the calm variant one can be bought each day (the page gives no prices).
- **Discarding.** The leader, co-leaders and elders can discard an untaken task; a new one appears after a short wait (the wiki says 30 minutes [W3]).
- **Points and prizes.** Checkpoints along a track each unlock one prize choice per member; members who completed no task, or left, get nothing; the top three also get placement prizes; diamonds reshuffle prize choices. Ties go to who scored sooner.
- **Leagues.** Five leagues; each allows more tasks and prizes than the one below. The top three groups go up; the bottom three go down, and so does any group that did not reach the first checkpoint or had nobody taking part. Others stay. The top league has no promotion; instead a global table ranks groups by their average over the last four weeks, from points per taking-part player, a bonus for larger groups, a placement bonus, and a deduction for tasks discarded per player.
- **Matching.** With up to 14 others in the same league "of a similar size", size meaning members who opted in, not members; in higher leagues a group may meet groups with one more or one fewer taking part. Joining or opting in after the start waits for the next race, so every group starts with a fixed number of eligible players.
- **Variants.** Themed weeks, among them a calm week with no promotion or relegation and easier tasks (the publisher's signatures, see section 6).

### 2.2 What a small or half-active group experiences

- **The goal scales with taking-part members.** The wiki's checkpoint table grows roughly in step with the number of opted-in members (REPORTED [W3]). So a group of 8 active members in a group of 30 races other groups of about 8: the 22 idle members cost nothing.
- **Very small groups cannot reach the last prizes.** The wiki notes that groups with fewer than 4 taking part cannot reach some checkpoints even with only the best tasks, "a known limitation" (REPORTED [W3]).
- **Doing nothing has a cost.** A group with nobody taking part drops a league (VERIFIED [S11]).

### 2.3 How it changed, and why

- **First, groups as places to talk and trade** (the year, 2014 in the first draft, is not stated in either interview: unverified). "Neighbourhoods were just a place to talk and perhaps do some trading" (Camilla Avellar, former designer; REPORTED, PocketGamer.biz, 21 June 2022 [P1]).
- **First try: a leaderboard that went wrong.** "We introduced a leaderboard, but we had not fully thought through the design aspects of it"; players "were progressing in ways that we didn't want them to"; a rushed fix "made it even worse than before"; lesson: "don't make any drastic changes or decisions in the moment" (Stephan Demirdjian, former game lead; REPORTED, mobilegamer.biz, 21 June 2022 [P2]).
- **About 2015: the weekly race.** It "gave neighbourhoods a reason to play together collaboratively but also with competition in mind" (Demirdjian [P1]); "It's a big risk adding competition to a nice, casual farming game. But it really paid off" (Avellar [P2]). Press dated the launch to March 2015 (search extract only).
- **September 2015: leagues** with top-three promotion and bottom-three relegation, opting out of the next race, and showing members' points (REPORTED, IBTimes; dated by its linked forum threads [P3]).
- **Later: the top league with a global table** (search extract only, a winter update); today its score subtracts discarded tasks per player (VERIFIED [S11]). INFERENCE: the deduction exists because officers were cycling the board to fish for easy, high-point tasks.

### 2.4 Problems players report (REPORTED, individual complaints, search extracts only)

Players kicked after finishing their tasks but before prizes; chat full of members "being kicked out or called out" that made a player "dread logging in"; groups recruiting with their own weekly minimums [R1].

## 3. The roadside shop and its newspaper

### 3.1 The rules (VERIFIED on the support portal [S13], unless marked)

- Unlocks at level 7. Sell from your storage; each box holds up to 10 of one item; set the price; put on sale.
- **Price.** The wiki says the price runs from 1 coin up to 3.6 times the item's default value (REPORTED [W4]); the support page says only "adjust the price".
- **Advertising.** List a box in the shared newspaper once every 5 minutes, or skip the wait for 1 diamond. Each player gets their own copy of the paper, refreshed every few minutes (REPORTED [W5]).
- **Taking items back** now costs diamonds, rising with use (2, then 10, then 20), resetting after 14 days without a return. (The wiki still says items cannot be taken back: it is out of date here.)
- **Daily limit.** At most 80 expansion and upgrade materials bought from other players per day. The wiki dates it to an October 2019 update (REPORTED [W4]).
- **Hired helper.** An errand character, hired for diamonds by the day, searches other players' shops for the cheapest offer of an item and fetches it; he naps two hours after each errand (VERIFIED [S14]).
- **Bans.** Using third-party software leads to "a permanent ban of your Roadside Shop" (VERIFIED [S13]). Buying "in-game items or currencies from unauthorised sources" and selling accounts are against the rules (VERIFIED [S15], [S16]).

### 3.2 How it is abused

- **Trading for real money uses the shop.** A reseller site sells Hay Day coins and items for US dollars and says "We utilize the Roadside shop for transactions" (VERIFIED, read today [X1]). INFERENCE: a 1-coin floor lets a seller hand goods to a named buyer for nothing; a high ceiling lets a buyer hand coins to a seller by buying junk at the top price. Either way the shop becomes a transfer between two players who agreed elsewhere.
- **Arranged swaps** through forums, using a 1-coin "signal" listing (REPORTED [W4]): harmless in itself, the same mechanism.
- **Bots and modded clients** (the reason for the shop ban [S13]).
- **What the publisher did** (as far as visible): a daily limit on the scarcest goods, rising costs for taking items back, a shop ban for third-party software, account penalties for buying from outside. I found no published statement of why the daily limit came in. INFERENCE: limiting the scarce goods per day caps how much one account can funnel to another.

**For this game (INFERENCE).** Our rules forbid real money going between players and forbid anything that reads as real trading (L4), and the server must not trust a client with other people's data (L5c). A player-set price between two named players is exactly the channel the resellers use. Section 6 lists a safe form.

**Name collision to flag.** Hay Day also has a "Yard Sale Event" in which non-player visitors buy decorations from the farm (VERIFIED [S17]). "Yard sale" is plain English and free to use, but an event in which visitors buy a player's spare decorations would echo that game. The researcher on yard sales should know.

## 5. What keeps players in groups, and how groups fail

### 5.1 What publishers and analysts say keeps players

- **A shared weekly goal turns a chat room into a reason to return** (Hay Day's team, 2.3). A social feature "has its own loop" and outlasts a content update (Demirdjian [P2]).
- **Groups help players find people like them.** Guilds help players find communities "with similar playing styles, interests, and levels of engagement" (GameRefinery, 7 November 2019 [A1]).
- **Casual games add them in steps.** First chat and simple ask-and-give, a few months later shared goals and team contests (GameRefinery, 30 July 2019 [A2]; [A1]).
- **Three hooks: something to grow, a reason to return each day, and obligation to the group.** Naavik (Becky Matthew, 8 September 2022 [A3]): groups work through progression (a group level), return triggers (daily giving limits, events) and social obligation; "Social pressures increase spending behavior and engagement". INFERENCE: this last mechanism is also the source of the pressure complaints; we should use the first two hard and the third gently.
- **Contributor rewards, not membership rewards.** Every one of the systems read rewards only members who did something (Hay Day, Township, Gardenscapes, Royal Match: VERIFIED above).

### 5.2 How groups fail

| Failure | Evidence | What publishers did |
|---|---|---|
| **Dead groups** (leader gone, nobody active) | Hay Day reassigns only after 30 days, and never if there is no co-leader or elder [S2]; players in other games ask for dead guilds to be pruned (REPORTED, player forums [R2]) | Automatic succession (14, 30, 60 days by game); activity defined as playing, not opening the app [G1]; removal after 180 days idle [G2]; activity levels shown on teams [T1] |
| **Big groups win, small groups lose** | — | Matching by number of taking-part members [S12], [T3]; minimum size to enter (Royal Match: 10) [D2] |
| **Task hoarding and board cycling** | Officers discarding tasks to fish for easy ones (INFERENCE from the penalty) | A deduction per discarded task on the top table [S11]; Township does not allow reserving tasks [T2] (whether it once did is not verified) |
| **Pressure and kick disputes** | Kicked after doing the work, before prizes; chats full of call-outs; group-set point minimums (REPORTED [R1]) | A calm no-relegation week (Hay Day [S10]); points of kicked members still count [S9]; in Township nobody can exclude a member from the race [T2], whereas Hay Day's leaders and co-leaders can opt members out [S2] |
| **Hopping between groups** for prizes | — | Frozen roster at the start [S9], [S12]; waits that grow with each leave or kick [T1], [E1]; no re-entry into the current event [D2] |
| **Chat moderation** | Supercell runs a filter on every line and name and still asks players to report [S6]; muting [S7]; Township removed private messages and mail [T4] | Family-friendly groups with stricter filters [S5]; under-16s only in those [S5]. Our own policy research already rules out free chat (yard-policy.md, "No free chat") |
| **Cheaters** | Township bars a detected cheater from the race, may deduct their points, and drops the team from top tables [T5] | (our server checks, 7.5) |
| **A leaderboard changed in a hurry** | "made it even worse than before" (Hay Day [P2]) | Change contest rules slowly, tested (Demirdjian's lesson) |

## 6. For the designers

Each player runs their own yard; a company is a group of up to 30 sharing a weekly order (specification 7.2, D6); currency packs are now allowed (ruling 13). This is advice; the designers and the lead decide.

### 6.1 Eight mechanics worth having, in our own form

**1. A company office in every member's yard.** Joining puts a small office (or a flag on the weighbridge cabin) in the yard, with the emblem and the week's order board; a visitor on a tour can tap it to see the company and ask to join. *Why:* the model's group lives on the farm, seen every session. *Trap:* it must not take working space or become a second screen of chores. *Common:* a group building on the home base (Hay Day, Township, SimCity BuildIt); the house, its mouse and its names are Hay Day's own.

**2. Finding a company that suits you.** A suggested list showing emblem, kind, members, a plain activity level (quiet, steady, busy) computed by the server from accepted rules actions, and a mark where the average rung matches yours; dormant companies left out. *Why:* groups keep players by matching "similar playing styles" [A1]; a newcomer in a dead company leaves. *Trap:* no typed search terms or tags; no individual's activity shown to strangers. *Common:* Township's activity level, Hay Day's match icons.

**3. A weekly order with a frozen roster and fair matching.** Before the week, each member is "in" or "out" (default: in if they played last week); the roster is fixed at the start; the order's lines scale per member who is in; league tables group companies with about the same number in; a newcomer joins next week's order. *Why:* a company of six can then compete with a company of thirty, and hopping gains nothing. *Trap:* the specification sizes the order by all members with seven days of accepted play; it should be those who said "in", or idle members make the order unreachable. Our tiers (50, 80, 100, 125 %) already act as checkpoints. *Common:* Hay Day, Township, Royal Match. Our numbers.

**4. Claimed lines with a deadline and a weekly allowance.** One claim at a time ("I can cover copper."); a claim lapses back to the board if its lot has not arrived by its deadline; a weekly number of claims per member; officers may set aside an unclaimed line at a cost to the company's league standing, and a new one appears later. *Why:* stops one member sitting on the good lines and officers cycling the board. *Trap:* a lapse costs only the time, never a fine; the allowance is never extendable with currency. *Common:* take-one boards with timers and discards (Hay Day, Township, SimCity BuildIt); the horse, track, horseshoes and themed weeks are Hay Day's signature. Our lines are grades of metal filled by real lots.

**5. Leagues with promotion and relegation, and a calm week.** Five to seven tiers named in our world (for example by licence class); top three up, bottom three down; down also for a company that reached no tier with members in; once a month a "maintenance week" without relegation and with smaller lines. *Why:* something to climb for months; the calm week answers the pressure complaints. *Trap:* members who said "out" never cause relegation; no league is bought. *Common:* top-three up, bottom-three down (Hay Day, Township, SimCity BuildIt). A named calm week is close to one publisher's variant: ours is monthly and tied to yard maintenance.

**6. Help requests between members, paid at the board price.** A member posts one need at a time on a cooldown ("need 2 t of No.1 copper"); others fill it from their bays in small lots; the requester pays the posted price of the hour, settled by the server as a normal sale; a daily give cap; the helper earns standing. *Why:* the commonest group mechanic (all games in section 4) and the daily reason to open the company. *Trap:* not a transfer channel: no player-set price, no gift of currency, nobody outside the company, nothing above a day's normal sale. *Common:* yes.

**7. A weekly line of recognition.** Each Monday the company page shows positive counts per member for the past week (lines covered, tonnes sent, requests filled) and a few marks in our own words ("Steady supplier", "First to cover"). *Why:* shows who carried the week without a leader saying it. *Trap:* never rank the bottom, never show "did nothing", never list who said "out": the reported call-outs come from public shaming. *Common:* weekly counts (Township, Royal Match); Hay Day's title names are its own.

**8. Roles, succession, leaving and removal that keep rewards fair.** Leader, up to three co-leaders, members; leadership passes after 14 days without a rules action (decided); a removed member waits before rejoining that company, longer each time; a member removed mid-week **keeps** the contributor reward for what they delivered; leaving and joining any company carries a short growing wait. *Why:* the commonest kick dispute is losing earned prizes; waits stop hopping. *Trap:* officers cannot opt others out (Township allows nobody to exclude a member [T2]; Hay Day lets officers do it [S2]); removal must not be a last-hour weapon. *Common:* roles and succession; keeping the removed member's reward improves on the model.

### 6.2 What not to take

- **Free chat:** ruled out by our policy research (yard-policy.md); the model needs a filter on every line, reports, mutes and moderators [S6], [S7].
- **A stand with player-set prices between named players**, or any send-to-a-player at a chosen price: the reseller channel [X1]. If yard sales involve other players, sales go to simulated buyers or at the board price within a band and caps, resolved by the server.
- **Buying progress in the group contest or in group help.** The model sells an extra task and paid prize reshuffles [S10], skips of the help-request wait and an extra daily request through its paid season pass [S3], and task swaps and extra tasks in its seasonal group event [S4]. With currency packs on sale, currency must never buy a claim, an allowance, a deadline, points, a tier or a reroll (7.5 already keeps purchases off ranked fields).
- **Random or rerolled prizes:** choosing among shown prizes is fine; a paid reshuffle reads like a loot box.
- **Lines sized by the strongest member** (Hay Day builds tasks from the highest level [S10]; INFERENCE: newcomers are shut out). Size each line to what several "in" members can supply.
- **Losing earned prizes on removal, and prizes for the top three only.** Reward every contributor by the tier reached.
- **Raids or wars on other yards** (SimCity BuildIt's Club Wars [E1]).
- **Anything signed by one publisher:** the horse race, track, horseshoes, themed weeks, the newspaper and its name, the errand boy, the request birdhouse, the neighbourhood house, the generosity titles, league names and colours, task and checkpoint tables, and numbers (5 to 9 tasks, 15 groups, 8-hour requests, 40 donations, 3.6 × price).

### 6.3 Questions for the lead

1. Size the order by members who said "in" rather than all members with seven days of play (item 3)? Research says yes.
2. Bring help requests (item 6) into stage 5? Every comparable game has them.
3. Let a removed member keep the week's contributor reward (item 8)? Research says yes.
4. Confine player-facing yard sales to simulated buyers or the board price (section 3)? Research says yes.

### Added (what would change a decision)

1. **The model sells group capacity for premium currency in five places; the first draft's summary named two.** An extra contest task, prize reshuffles [S10]; skipping the help-request wait and an extra daily request through the paid season pass [S3]; swapping and buying extra tasks (for diamonds or an ad) in the January 2026 shared-tree event [S4], [S18]. Since the owner has now put currency packs on sale, the design's rule "purchases and ads never touch ranked fields" (yard-design 7.5) must also cover **the company's shared work**: no purchase or ad buys a claim, a request, a cooldown skip, a task swap or an extra line. Section 6.2 now says so.
2. **The 30 is the publisher's own stated number** (store text), so telling the owner "30 is Hay Day's number" is safe to say as fact.
3. **The newest Hay Day group feature is cooperative, not competitive.** The January 2026 event has the whole group grow one shared object from members' ordinary daily work, with personal rewards too [S4], [S18]. INFERENCE: this supports a quiet, all-contribute company goal beside the weekly order, and it is a counterweight to the contest-first reading of section 2. The shared growing object and its tree are that publisher's own event: a company goal in our world must have its own form (for example, a company plant the members' deliveries build), not a tree.
4. **Hay Day lets officers opt members out of the contest** [S2]; Township forbids excluding anyone [T2]. The design's choice (officers cannot opt others out, 6.1 item 8) is the stricter one and matches Township, not the model.
5. **A note for whoever reads these pages next:** Supercell's support articles are rendered by script; a fetch tool shows only the title. Download the page and read the article text it embeds, or the claims look unsupported when they are not.
