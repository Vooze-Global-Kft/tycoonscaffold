### 2.1 The engine step: from rung 3 the run is the rule, watched or not (rulings 10.4 as amended, 14.1 to 14.9; D87; E42, E43; as built) **[26 Nov, after the attack]**

**How it came to be.** `engine2-group` built crew trips grouped out of the boss's sight (`notes/engine2-group.md`, 03:40). An engineer who wrote none of it attacked it (`attack/engine2/`; seven findings, each with its ruling in `attack/engine2/ruled.json`): a tick equals a jump on the whole state, an absence cut into many passes equals one pass at a fixed rung, 934 actions pressed inside runs broke nothing, mass is exact, absences and a clock set back pay once, an old save loads and the state stays bounded; but "the unwatched yard supplied 6 to 17 % more tonnes over three days than the watched one. A yard that does better when nobody looks is wrong in this game." The lead does not go back to single trips (the bench would be about 290 ms and the lever that keeps time away cheap in later stages would be gone). Instead the run is made the rule of the yard from rung `group.fromRung` (3), whoever is looking (ruling 14.1, E42). It was fixed in one pass by `engine2-fixA` (14.1, 14.3, 14.7, 14.8, 14.9; `notes/engine2-fixA.md`) and `engine2-fixB` (14.4, 14.5, 14.6, 14.10, 14.11; `notes/engine2-fixB.md`). This section is the rule as built, in the builders' sentences, under ruling 14; where G1 to G3 (`notes/engine2-LEAD.md`) differ, ruling 14 is right (it replaces G2). Files: `src/engine/salvage/{group.ts, crew.ts, truck.ts, round.ts, hand.ts, tick.ts, clock.ts, machines.ts, things.ts, scales.ts, types.ts, save.ts, rate.ts, bays.ts, away.ts, ladder.ts}`, `src/config/schema.salvage.ts`, `src/config/salvage.content.ts`; tests `runs.test.ts`, `group.test.ts`, `tickjump.test.ts`, `split.test.ts`, `awaybench.test.ts`; the gate's driver `scripts/content/yard-g2.ts`.

**2.1.1 The rule** (14.1 (a)). From rung `group.fromRung` (3) every cutter's and yard hand's work is a RUN, whoever is looking. A run starts at a departure where the first yard's order chooses the trip (for a yard hand: S12's order with P11 and E35 as changed by the floor-first rule of 2.1.14 and the owner's review, `yard-stage1.md` 13; for a cutter: the drop-off first, then the floor, best score first). It has a kind (a feed from one pile to one machine; pieces from one pile; one out tray; loose things at one place; whole floor things to one destination; a whole thing at one place; for a cutter: things at one place), which fixes its place and its destination, and a start t0 (that departure). The kind, the job and t0 are kept on the member in the state (`course`). Below `fromRung` there are no runs: every trip is chosen alone (the first yard). L16 stands: the hour the owner played stays as measured.

**2.1.2 The next trip of a run** (14.1 (a)). At every later departure inside a run the member takes the next trip of the same kind, from the same place to the same destination, by the planner's own in-place choice (a yard hand: the best pieces that fit his load from the same pile, the same tray's grams, the next loose or whole thing at the same place, the next whole floor trip to the same destination, the hopper's room at that departure for a feed; a cutter: the best-scored thing at the same place), with the first yard's presence rules applied at that departure, if that trip would end (a yard hand's drop, a cutter's cut) no later than t0 + `group.handRun_s` (30 s) for a yard hand, t0 + `group.cutRun_s` (120 s) for a cutter. When there is none, or it would end later, the run is over: the first yard's order chooses and a new run starts there. The first trip of a run always goes, however long. A run whose member finds nothing to do ends; so does every run when he is idle. How long a stopped machine can now wait in sight (measured, `runs.test.ts` (a)): until the run he is on ends, at most `handRun_s` (30 s on the real content) after the run's start, or the run's first trip if that is longer; another yard hand who is free takes it sooner (one hand: the full wait). Out of sight the same (the plan is the same run).

**2.1.3 In sight trip by trip, out of sight planned ahead** (14.1 (b), (c)). Out of the boss's sight at a departure (no fresh boss within `reach.sight` of the start, the source or the destination; a cutter: of him or of the place) and with `group.ahead` true, the REST of the run is planned there: its trips by the rule of 2.1.2 as the yard stands at that departure, each claimed, each with its own two leg ends, all ending by t0 + the limit (measured from the run's start, not from this departure), with one dated end; at that end every trip lands in order (each weigh-in dated at its own drop with its own day's prices; each thing cut whole, its pieces on its place's pile). The driver's trip out of sight is planned in the same way (its seven phases, one dated end). IN SIGHT each trip is an ordinary trip of the first yard: its own leg ends, claims for the trip under way only, landing at its own drop, chosen at its own departure by 2.1.2. Nothing a person can see is claimed ahead. With `group.ahead` false every run is made trip by trip out of sight too (the tests' and the bots' comparison). "Within sight": a fresh boss within `reach.sight` (10 m) of the run's start point, its sources or its destination (a place counts by its footprint; `group.ts bossSees`); away no boss is fresh.

What a planned run holds (as built by `engine2-group`, kept by the fix):
- **Yard hand**: each trip keeps its own two leg ends (walk + handle, walk back + handle, as a leg; `RunLoad {claims, spot, grade, pickAt, dropAt}`). At the run's end every trip lands in order: picked up, then weighed in as its own weigh-in (each grade line rounded once, the ticket dated at that trip's drop with that day's prices) or fed (what the hopper cannot take is weighed in at once). A run that claims an out tray restarts a machine stopped by that full tray at once (the claimed grams are his from the departure).
- **Cutter**: each thing is walked to and cut whole; at the end each is cut in order, its pieces on its place's pile (`RunCut {thing, walkEnd, cutEnd}`).
- **Driver**: the seven phases of a trip (walk, out, hook, back, weigh, tip, park) are planned at departure with the durations stage 1 gives them (`truck.trip.plan`, `TripPlan {t0, ends[7], x, y}`), one dated end at the park. At the end the phases are replayed at their planned instants: the hook paid (it was earmarked since departure) and the empty skip set down dated at the hook's instant (if it would already be full, it is full at the landing), the bridge ticket dated at the weigh's end, the load tipped, the truck parked. Not planned: the driver taking over a loaded truck the boss left (R2), rare; any trip while `group.ahead` is false.

**2.1.4 Opening a planned run; what is never planned** (14.1 (c), 14.3, 14.9). A planned run the boss comes within sight of is OPENED at that instant: what its plan finished by then lands in order, the trip under way goes on as one ordinary leg with its planned times, the rest is released; the member keeps his run (kind, place, destination and t0) and goes on with it trip by trip by 2.1.2. A run begun in sight whose member departs out of sight plans only its rest (to t0 + the limit). The leg under way, as built: a hand walking to a trip: a walk-to leg with its claims; walking back: the trip is picked now and he walks back; a cutter cutting: a work leg, whole, as decided; walking to a thing: a walk-to leg whose end decides by stage 1's sight rule; the truck: the phase under way. Opening is checked at every live instant and at every presence sample (`hand.ts bossStep`). No action opens a run: nothing a run holds can be taken by anyone else (claims), and presence is the only opener. The merchant's round is away `merchant.round_s` in every presence state (there is no long round; 2.1.9). A rate machine takes one hopper piece at a time in every presence state (the machines' grouped segment is removed: it depended on sight and saved nothing; a saved segment, `work.n`, still finishes). Never planned: the drop-off, skips filling, purchases arriving, the crucible, the day, the merchant's round, the rate machines; in stage 2 also visitors, the van, lots and the crate (each one event already). A back-field plate drawing from a bay (2.8.1) runs in segments that end only at the events of 2.8.1 (L8), in every presence state. **The magnet crane** (2.7) moves move by move, one dated move at a time (`WorksState.move`, 2.18), in every presence state: exact, the same present and away. Not built: its run planned ahead out of sight (ruling 14.1's form; `notes/s2c-join2b.md` 6). **[27 Nov, ruling 20.5]**

**2.1.5 The state** (14.1 (d)). `CrewMember.course?: { t0, job, kind }` (the run he is on; kept through a planned run, its opening and a save; `RunKind`: feed, pile, tray, loose, floorWhole, whole, cut), `CrewMember.run?` (a planned run: `{ t0, end, start, k, legS, dest?, loads?, cuts? }` as before), `truck.trip.plan?` as before. All optional and bounded; a stage-1 save has none and loads as it was. A saved course is kept when well formed (a kind its member's role makes, its machines known, its t0 not after the save's clock), else dropped (he chooses by the first yard's order at his next departure). A saved planned run is kept when well formed and bounded (at most 64 trips or things, finite instants in order, string claims; its end never before the save's clock); a broken one is dropped, its member idle where the run began and its claims released; a broken plan restarts the trip from its walk. The rules that differ with presence on purpose (pieces only while the boss is in the yard, the floor's whole things, the foreman, the hand stripper, a cutter's piece-by-piece cut within sight of the boss) are untouched and apply inside runs at each departure (14.1 (e)).

**2.1.6 Where the numbers live; the switch** (14.1 (f)). The numbers of `group` are content, not code: `src/config/salvage.content.ts` `group: { fromRung: 3, handRun_s: 30, cutRun_s: 120, ahead: true }`, schema `group: GroupSchema.optional()` in `src/config/schema.salvage.ts` (all four fields required inside the block), written into game.json by `build-yard.py`. A content without `group` has no runs, and a hand-made partial block (tests) reads a missing field as no runs, single trips, ahead (so no number lives in `group.ts`). `group.ahead` is the switch for tests and the bots (false: every run is made trip by trip even out of sight; it also stops the driver's trip from being planned). `group.fromRung` beyond the ladder (99): no runs at all (the first yard's rules). `group.roundAway_s` and `group.maxRun_s` do not exist (14.3; the attacker's drivers that still pass `roundAway_s` are ignored).

**2.1.7 What is promised** (G1 as amended by 14.1). (a) A tick equals a jump, exactly, on the whole state, runs and the truck's plan included, in every presence state; (b) the same function runs present and away. It is NOT promised that a yard with the boss present but out of sight equals the same yard with the boss away: the first yard's rules make them differ on purpose, and these differences are the rule (B3, 14.1 (e)):
1. While the boss is in the yard, yard hands carry pieces only (P11 of the first yard); whole things that need no cutting (tier 0) are cleared from the tipping floor only when he is away or the floor is over half full (E35, `crew.ts FLOOR_CLEAR_SHARE`).
2. The foreman's boost runs only live (`clock.ts boostOn`); the hand stripper works only with the boss (`machines.ts`); a cutter within sight of the boss cuts piece by piece.
3. Hold's lot (2.2) is set aside only while the boss is away.
The RUN is not a difference: watched or not the member makes the same run (2.1.1, 2.1.2); what differs is only whether its rest is planned ahead, a shortcut held by the gate of 2.1.11. The checks are not a difference either: they follow the state (2.1.10). A tagged thing is not a difference: its owner is decided by sight, present and away alike (2.12, E4, B15). The built test "present equals away" (`group.test.ts`) runs on a content where the presence rules above cannot apply (no cable and no loose things in any source); it stays as a test of that content and promises nothing beyond (a) and (b).

**What the screen shows.** While a planned run holds a member, his `phase`, `from`, `to`, `t0`, `t1`, `carries` (a cutter's `cut`) are set to the leg of his plan under way at the end of every advance (tick, jump and away alike), a pure function of plan and clock, so the frame draws him walking and cutting as before; the truck's phase, `t0`, `endsAt` and position likewise. The queue reads the run's end (`run.end`, `trip.plan.ends[6]`), never these fields. Two things the frame cannot see from them (`requests.md`, `[engine2-group]` to the screens): a yard hand walking back on a run has `load` empty (his pieces stay claimed until the run lands), so the barrow is drawn loaded when `run && phase === 'walkBack'` (grade in `carries`); the truck on a planned trip has `skip` null until it lands, so a full skip is drawn from phase `back` on. In sight a member now repeats one kind of trip for up to 30 s; the frame may draw a run's kind from `course` (nothing reads it there yet).

**2.1.8 What planning ahead moves, and what the rule itself moves.** A planned run is not exact against the same run made trip by trip, and this is the shortcut, not a tolerance: (1) a planned run's work becomes visible to the rest of the yard at its end (or its opening), not at each trip's own drop: the merchant, the crucible and the floor's room see it up to a run later; (2) its later trips are chosen from what waits at its departure (pieces the cutter drops during the run, a tray that fills during a floor run, a level or the foreman's boost begun during a run, wait for the next run); (3) a skip set down by a planned hook that would already be full when the trip lands is full at the landing; (4) a feed trip whose hopper is fuller at landing than planned weighs the rest in at once. Measured by `engine2-fixA` (section 4): "The yard is chaotic: a change of one part in a billion in the cutters' power moves one absence's net by up to 4 % (7 of 10 within 2 %)". "HANDS' PLANS cost a little systematically: 9 of 10 states lower, mean -3.5 % on one absence" (the plan cannot see the pieces a cutter drops meanwhile, so later loads are lighter, and a hopper fed at the end runs dry meanwhile). "CUTTERS' PLANS move a single absence both ways (-5 % to +7 %): a 120 s plan claims every thing of the place for the whole run, so a second cutter finds nothing there". What was tried to bring plan and trip-by-trip closer (landing at the yard's own instants, a fair share of claims, full loads only) made the gap larger and is not in the code (14.9).

### 2.2 Hold sells only what the next load needs, and stays on (E40 as amended; rulings 10.5, 14.6, 14.11; D88; H1; as built) **[26 Nov, after the attack]**
