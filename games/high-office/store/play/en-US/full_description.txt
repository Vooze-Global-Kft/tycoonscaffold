An invented country and a comedy desk: push each paper up to sign it, or pull it down into the drawer marked FOR FURTHER STUDY.

You are the new mayor of Dithering-on-the-Dawdle, in the Republic of Middling (motto: Proudly Adequate). You were elected unopposed. The other candidate went to the wrong hall.

Visitors queue at your door. A bus spotter would like the 7:02 named after you. A pumpkin grower asks for the freedom of the town for her pumpkin, which has asked very politely. A lobbyist leaves a small fruit basket, for no reason. Each paper prints both outcomes before you touch it, in exact numbers: what signing costs or pays, which club it pleases and which it annoys, and whether the poll moves. Those are the numbers applied. A signed paper rests on the out-tray for two seconds; tap it to take it back.

The town's opinion belongs to its hobby clubs: the Bus Spotters' Society, the Shop Window Guild and the Giant Vegetable Society, with a brass band, anglers, kite flyers and a hedge committee further up. Their approval multiplies everything your ministries earn, from x0.4 at 0 % to x1.6 at 100 %. A club that falls below 20 boycotts its ministry until you settle with it or 8 more papers are decided. Let the gossip pile up and the town paper prints a front page about you: apologise on the steps, or say nothing and collect a fifth less for 10 papers.

A term is a fixed number of papers. From 60 % of the way through you may call the election. The poll board shows exactly how each ward would vote today, and on election night a clerk chalks up the wards, safest first. Win, and your Name Recognition grows; with enough of it, and a record of ministry levels to match, you may stand for Governor of Upper Wobbling, then President in Middlingham, taking your ministers with you. Lose, and nothing you built is lost: you carry on as Acting Mayor, and the winner resigns to spend more time with her hat.

Taxes, at least, need no signature, which makes this technically an idle game. Four ministries in town (six as Governor, eight as President) collect dibs every second. With the app shut they go on collecting at 35 % of the rate, for a maximum of 4 hours, and a visitor leaves a paper every 6 minutes, until 10 wait in the in-tray. Moods, gossip, the poll and the term wait for you to read.

At the desk a visitor comes about every 12 seconds. When nobody is waiting, the Treasury Clerk slides a budget slip across, and a flick up buys what she advises: a level or a new ministry. Nine ministers apply for posts along the way; each runs one ministry and brings one desk rule, printed on every paper it touches. The levels go into the Ledger, a clothbound book kept for the tycoon in you.

Everything here is invented: the country, its towns and clubs, its politicians, companies and newspapers. There are no parties, and nobody can type a name into the game.

Watching an ad is always your choice, made from a button that names its reward: half again on the away report; Check the Sofa, a small sum once a term; Call the Spin Doctor, which ends the cost of saying nothing; Send a Courier, which ends a boycott; Open the Doors, which fills the in-tray. No banners appear, and no ad plays between screens. Five purchases, none of them random: Junior Clerk on Duty (the Standing Order from your first day: the junior clerk buys the advised ministry level while you play), Long Lunch (time away counts for 6 hours, not 4), the Walnut & Brass Desk Set and Gilt Portrait Frames (both looks only), and Skip the Ad Videos: the Private Secretary sits through them, and the reward arrives at once. From the moment you call an election until election night ends, no ad or purchase is offered. No ad or purchase moves a mood or a poll point: those move only by papers you decide.
